§ The Problem

The injustice of unverifiable translation.

Under India's legal framework (BNSS §173, §180, §313), citizen statements must be recorded and read back to the informant before they sign them. On paper, the protection exists.

In practice, the workflow is entirely human and fundamentally lossy. When a citizen speaks in a low-resource regional dialect — Marwari, Dhundhari, Bagri — the intake officer mentally translates and types the statement in standard Hindi or English.

What is lost: the original dialect vocabulary, culturally specific idioms, critical semantic nuances. And with them, the digital audit trail of what was actually said. The voice exists. The problem is that no one preserved it.

§ Real-Life Case Study

The FIR Translation Gap.

Step 01 — Complaint

A rural farmer from the Marwar district arrives at a police station to report a property encroachment. He speaks only colloquial Marwari.

Step 02 — Intake

The intake officer, without formal dialect training, listens and types the FIR in standard Hindi. The dialect-specific word for a traditional land boundary — Merh — has no direct Hindi equivalent.

Step 03 — The Error

The officer writes Deewar (a generic wall). The FIR is signed. The original audio of the farmer's words is never recorded or structured.

Step 04 — Consequence

Months later in trial, the defense argues the FIR (which says "wall") does not match the physical land record. The victim's true testimony is unrecoverable.

⚠ Outcome

Because no auditable link exists between the original spoken testimony and the written record, the case collapses on a translation error, not a lack of evidence.

"The problem isn't that the legal system lacks a mechanism for interpretation. The problem is that the mechanism is un-auditable."
— Core Design Premise, Sakshya-Vani

Cited Context

The Supreme Court of India has emphasized that the ability to understand and be understood in one's own language is an existential right. Studies across Indian police stations reveal a systemic lack of institutional framework for linguistic support at the point of intake, particularly for dialects outside the 8th Schedule.

§ How It Works

Dialect preserved. Record verified.

Sakshya-Vani is not a translation app. It is an evidence-preservation layer. The original audio is always the ground truth. The AI only proposes. The human verifies. Every step is logged.

"AI proposes. Human verifies. Original audio remains authoritative."

01

Immutable Capture

The citizen speaks. The raw audio is instantly cryptographically hashed (SHA-256), creating a tamper-evident chain of custody record.

02

Dialect Transcription

A fine-tuned ASR model generates a verbatim Devanagari transcript in the native dialect — preserving exact vocabulary, idioms, and phonetics.

03

Linked Translation

A Machine Translation model proposes a standard Hindi translation. Both transcripts coexist — neither replaces the other.

04

Dual-Pane Verification

The frontline worker clicks any Hindi sentence. The corresponding 5-second audio clip plays instantly. Errors are corrected before the record is finalized.

05

Audit Trail Export

The system exports a structured package: original audio, dialect transcript, Hindi translation, correction log, timestamps, and hash verification.

§ Technology

Built for accuracy. Engineered for the field.

IndicWhisper IndicTrans2 LoRA / QLoRA Silero VAD FastAPI Next.js Docker SHA-256 ULCA Compliant

Acoustic Adaptation

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (QLoRA) on Whisper/Conformer architectures, trained with SpecAugment to handle rural field noise and unstandardized phonology.

Meaning-Critical Evaluation

Beyond WER, we evaluate models on a custom Meaning-Critical Error Rate (MCER) — measuring how often the AI alters legally significant semantic meaning.

Dialect Preservation Score

A composite metric measuring survival of local idioms, named entities, and negations from source audio through the final transcript.

ULCA-Ready Packaging

API endpoints containerized via Docker, formatted to the Universal Language Contribution API schemas for seamless national-scale deployment.
